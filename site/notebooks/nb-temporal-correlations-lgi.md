# Testing Non-Classicality in Time Evolution: Leggett-Garg Inequalities and Hamiltonian Dynamics

Examines how Leggett-Garg inequalities and temporal correlation functions distinguish quantum from macrorealistic classical dynamics in Hamiltonian systems without requiring spatial Bell separation.

## Findings

When spatial Bell inequality tests are not feasible due to lack of space-like separation or independent observable pairs, non-classicality can be tested temporally via Leggett-Garg inequalities (LGIs) [source-04fcd05dc9f24c55]. In macroscopic double-well potentials governed by Hamiltonian equations, tunneling probabilities yield oscillations that violate macrorealism over broad ranges of decoherence [source-04fcd05dc9f24c55]. Furthermore, local probe time correlations and LGI violations can identify both finite-order and infinite-order (such as Kosterlitz-Thouless) quantum phase transitions in spin Hamiltonian models (e.g., XXZ and anisotropic XY chains), where singularities in time correlations and maximal LGI violations coincide with critical points [source-04fcd05dc9f24c55]. Additionally, theoretical formulations show that bipartite quantum non-locality and correlation structures extend beyond standard Bell-CHSH limits into multivariable graph expansions [source-c7d665600a6949f0].

## Limitations and competing views

Findings are based on preprint abstracts from arXiv (quant-ph); full experimental loophole considerations (e.g., invasiveness of measurements, clumping, and detection efficiency) require examining full experimental texts rather than theoretical abstracts.
